The Man  ·  The Vision  ·  The Work

I'm Powell Parker.
The Values That Shape My Life.

Cowboy. Film Producer. Screenwriter. Cattleman. Entrepreneur. Philanthropist. Father. Movie wrangler. Faith-driven West Texan transplanted deep into the Arizona desert. None of those titles tell the whole story alone — but together, they prove what happens when you commit completely to the work.

Where It All Started

Born in Hereford, Texas.
Built in the Arizona Desert.

I grew up in Hereford, Texas — a town where a man’s word is a binding contract. In the 80s we moved to Rocky, Oklahoma, a small farming and ranching town in Tornado Alley where I learned early what it means to weather hard storms and keep showing up.


I lived in Queen Creek, Arizona back when it was red dirt roads and cotton fields. I competed in High School and College Rodeos across the state in Team Roping and Bulldogging. The rodeo arena doesn't care about excuses — you either prepare, rate your steer, and execute, or you taste dirt.


I built my life around three things that never change: my faith, my family, and my word. Everything else — the screenplays, the film packaging, and the business operations — flows directly out of that foundation.

The Open Range • Pasture Gathering

Big Pastures & Heavy Skies — Gathering the Herds

You don’t understand true distance until you’re sitting horseback behind a couple hundred head of black cattle stretching across the green horizon. Whether pushing herds across the sprawling Sandhills of Nebraska or moving stock through high desert pastures, this is where real horsemanship begins. Reading flight zones, matching pace, and keeping a herd together through changing weather demands quiet patience.

Hand Mechanics • Desert Stockmanship

Building the Loop — Golden Hour Preparation

Swinging a rope in an arena is one thing; building a loop while navigating rocky washes, mesquite, and catclaw is another animal entirely. Your horse has to rate the target automatically while you read wind and brush clearance. It’s an art form honed until the loop becomes an extension of your hand. When you have to doctor a yearling in rough country, you make the first shot count.

Range Medicine • Two Ropes & Honest Trust

Pasture Doctoring — Holding the Tension

There are no squeeze chutes on the open range. When an animal comes down with foot rot or pneumonia miles from the pens, you rope and doctor them right where they stand. One rider on the head, one on the heels, ropes wrapped taut to the horn, and seasoned horses leaning into their britchen to hold the steer completely immobile. It demands total coordination and absolute faith in your horse.

Ranch Works • In the Dirt

Branding, Flanking & Processing — The Unvarnished Truth

Ranch life isn't just watching sunsets from a saddle; it’s stepping off into the dirt and wrestling 250-pound calves to the ground. In the branding pens, everyone has a job: heeler, flanker, vaccinator, iron man. Down on a knee holding a front leg and neck while the irons smoke and the medicine goes in, you earn every meal you eat. It’s hard, necessary labor that protects the herd.

In the Arena • Timed Precision

Me & Joel — Team Roping

That's me and my buddy Joel — Rubio the Palomino under me and Monty the Chestnut under him. Team roping takes the skills of the open range and puts them against the clock. It's not just a sport; it's a brotherhood. You learn fast that you can't do it alone — you have to trust your partner and your horse completely. I've been competing since my Queen Creek rodeo days, and it still keeps my edge sharp.

Arizona Roots

State 48.
My Home Forever.

I lived in Queen Creek, Arizona back when it was red dirt roads, cotton fields, and working spreads where everybody knew everybody. I competed in High School and College Rodeos all across the state in Team Roping and Bulldogging.
